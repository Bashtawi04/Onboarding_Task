# Ministry of Labour (MOL) digest: starting a job in Qatar

> **SYNTHETIC SUMMARY for training. Not an official MOL document, may be out of date.** A production agent must ingest the official sources (mol.gov.qa, Al Meezan almeezan.qa) instead.

This page gives the general legal background behind some of our onboarding rules. It is deliberately short and general. Al Dana's own rules (the ONB procedures and the handbook) are company policy and are sometimes more generous than the law.

## The main law
The main law governing employment is the **Qatar Labour Law No. 14 of 2004**. This digest does not quote articles or other laws.

## Contracts and probation
- Employment contracts are in writing.
- A probation period can be **at most 6 months**.

## Working hours
- Normal working hours are **8 hours a day and 48 hours a week**.
- During Ramadan, working hours are reduced to **6 hours a day and 36 hours a week**.

## Annual leave
- The statutory minimum annual leave is **3 weeks per year** for employees with less than 5 years' service, and **4 weeks per year** from 5 years' service.

## Payment of wages
- Workers' wages are paid by **bank transfer** under the **Wage Protection System (WPS)**. Employers use it so that salary payments are recorded electronically. This digest does not describe deadlines or penalties.

## Minimum wage
- A national minimum wage exists: **QAR 1,000 basic**, plus **QAR 300 for food and QAR 500 for housing** if these are not provided in kind by the employer.

## Personal data
- Qatar's personal data privacy law (**Law No. 13 of 2016**, Personal Data Privacy Protection) applies to employee and candidate data. Treat employee records as confidential and use them only for the purpose they were collected.

## Everything else
Requirements for visas, residence permits, the QID (Qatar ID card), medical fitness, health cards, fees, processing times and penalties are **not covered here**. HR or the PRO desk confirms the current official requirement with the Ministry of Labour or the relevant authority before giving an answer to an employee.
